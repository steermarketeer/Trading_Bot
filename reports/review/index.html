What the sideways study says (your concept, six coins, 4h + daily, audited)
  • The extreme is tested first, mostly: yes. After a pump of at least 2 ATR that goes sideways, the high is revisited before the range resolves in 75% of daily cases (77% on the 4h); the holdout agrees. Small legs at the same distance: 56 – 74%, so part of it is proximity.
  • The sweep of the high is not the exit. After a wick above the high that closes back below, the range still continues up 74% of the time on the daily (72% on the 4h), the same at a strong S/R level. The 16 Dec sweep of 4,099 was the 1-in-5 case. Exiting after a sweep of the trade's high loses on our trades.
  • The retest of the price is the key: supported. When the price comes back to the pump's close and holds (close no more than 0.25 ATR below), the range continues up 72% (daily) / 71% (4h); when the retest closes below, 55%; with no retest, 54%. Holdout: 87% vs 30% (daily). Here: the 21 Nov pump's close (3,358) was retested 22 – 26 Nov and held (26 Nov close 3,327), then the 27 Nov pump came.
  • RSI cooling at the retest does not help: a retest with RSI 5+ points lower continues 54%, a warm retest 71%. Most continuations break with a higher RSI than at the pump.

My read

  1. The target sat beyond the year's biggest liquidity. 4,099 was the high of the year; the price went to it twice, swept it on 16 Dec and closed back below. On this chart that close was the exit and the short. But across six coins a sweep like that continues up 3 times in 4, even at a strong level, so "exit at the sweep" is not a rule; what is wrong here is the fixed target (3 : 1 then, 5 : 1 now) sitting 585 above the year's high: your rule, not every trade has a fixed ratio.
  2. The closes gave the exit three times, each later and worse:
    · 16 Dec close back below 4,099 (sweep of the high): about +2.4R
    · 18 Dec close below the pullback low (failure to make a new high): about +1.0R
    · 19 Dec close below the 3,475 neckline (double top confirmed): about +0.2R.
  3. The short: from the 16 Dec close (3,985) to the liquidity below. Price reached 3,100 on 20 Dec, about 2.2× the risk to a stop above 4,110.

What I can and cannot turn into a rule: "take the exit on the close back below a known higher-timeframe high" is testable on all bounces whose target lies beyond such a high (the ceiling reason: 107 of 129 research trades have a ceiling before the target, win 54% vs 68% without).
