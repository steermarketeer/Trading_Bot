What price did
  1. 5 – 9 Dec: up to 2,408 (+0.96R).
  2. 10 – 11 Dec: a lower high (2,380), then the 11 Dec flush to 2,115, closing at 2,226. $45M of longs liquidated, the 3rd-largest day from June 2023 to February 2024. (The original v3.1 stop, 2,129, was taken here.)
  3. 12 – 26 Dec: range 2,117 – 2,346, holding above the stop.
  4. 27 – 28 Dec: new high 2,456 (+1.23R): the best the trade got.
  5. 3 Jan 2024, 12:00 – 13:00 UTC: one 1h candle from 2,310 to 2,067.2, closing at 2,249.6: a sweep that closed back above. $52.6M of longs liquidated, the 2nd-largest day of the season. Our stop at 2,071.59 was taken by $4.39.
  6. 10 – 12 Jan: 2,646 → 2,720. The target (2,760) was still not reached within the 60-day limit.

My read

  1. The stop was inside the liquidity, not beyond it. The 0.786 rule put it at 2,071.59, just above the S/R 2,068 and on the long-liquidation cluster. Price went to that liquidity twice (11 Dec: 2,115, 3 Jan: 2,067) and both times closed back up. The 3 Jan candle was a textbook sweep. A stop below the pool (under 2,019 – 2,025) would have survived both flushes.
  2. The target was a fixed 3R that happened to sit on the Fib 1.618 (2,761). Price peaked at 2,720. The real liquidity targets were the 1.272 (2,490, reached 10 Jan) or the 2,400 – 2,456 highs.
  3. The 4h close did not confirm right after the entry (2,231 < 2,244), and there was a 15m double top just after it.
  4. A daily failure exit would have been about −0.1R: peak 2,408 on 9 Dec, a lower high on 11 Dec, and the 11 Dec close (2,226) below the pullback low (2,323).

The question for you: on a sweep like 3 Jan, would you place the stop beyond the whole liquidity pool and accept a bigger risk, or wait for the sweep to close back and then enter (stop under the wick)?
