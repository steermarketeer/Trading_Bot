What I think of this trade, with Fibonacci and liquidity

  1. The direction was right; the stop was in the most obvious place. Our stop (1,799) sat just above equal lows at 1,794 / 1,794: the clearest sell-side liquidity on the chart, and only a 1h Fib 0.236 pullback away. A normal 0.382–0.5 pullback had to take it.
  2. The bigger liquidation pool was above us. About 6.6% of open interest in short liquidations sat between 1,830 and 1,940, against little long liquidation just under our stop. Price took the small pool under us first, then went for the big one above.
  3. Price moves from one pool of liquidity to the next. First the lows under our stop, then the 1,833 high, then the deeper lows at 1,775 / 1,768, then the daily high 1,849 and the 1.618 extension. The bot only read "breakout, go", not where the liquidity sat on both sides.
  4. We chased. Entry came after the push: 1h RSI about 70, volume 5 times average, price at the upper Bollinger band. Bigger players sell into that and buy back lower, at liquidity.
  5. A Fibonacci pullback alone would also have lost. A limit buy at the 0.382 / support confluence (1,784, filled 12 Jul 00:05) with a stop under the 1h Fib 0.618 (1,755) was stopped on 13 Jul at 17:55 (low 1,748), a day before the run to 1,897. The level that held was below the next liquidity: the 1,737 low, which is also the 1h Fib 0.786. A stop under 1,737, entry 1,784 and target at the 1.618 extension (1,899) would have worked (+2.1R), but I'm reading that off the chart afterwards. It has to be tested on all trades before we trust it.

Rule idea to test: for a breakout long, enter on a pullback into Fibonacci 0.382–0.5 that lines up with support or liquidity; put the stop below the next untaken liquidity pool beyond the 0.618 (not just above the nearest one); take profit at the next buy-side liquidity or the 1.272 / 1.618 extension.
